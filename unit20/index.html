A · FGSM in 2D

drag epsilon

A tiny linear classifier, two hardcoded weights, one point. The boundary splits the plane into class A and class B. Drag epsilon and the point steps toward the corner of its own epsilon box — in the direction of the model's gradient sign, nothing else.

epsilon (perturbation budget)0
model A says: class A — correct
WHY THIS FIRST FGSM is one line of math: step every coordinate by epsilon · sign(gradient). No training, no search — the point just walks straight to a corner of the epsilon box. That one line is the ancestor of every gradient attack in this course.

B · Transfer

same perturbation, a model you never touched

Same point, same move — fired blind at a second classifier with a differently angled boundary. No gradient from this model was ever computed. Watch whether its label flips anyway.

model B says: class A — correct
HONEST LABEL Both boundaries, both weights, and the gradient step are exact math from hardcoded numbers — nothing here is a trained model, and nothing is randomised. It's a toy built to make the mechanism tangible. The real transfer number, on a real pair of models, is the Lab below.
